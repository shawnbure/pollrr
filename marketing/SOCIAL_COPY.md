## Launch-before-launch founder post

Most online polls give you a percentage and hide everything that would make it trustworthy.

I’m building Pollrr for creators and publishers who want better receipts:

- people answer before seeing the result;
- human votes stay separate from AI estimates;
- sampling and distribution sources are labeled;
- optional explanations show why each side answered;
- aggregate results get a verification record.

Creating polls and collecting responses is free. AI helps write neutral questions and explain verified results—but AI never gets counted as a voter.

We’re inviting a small first group of creators to run real questions with real audiences. If you publish about politics, local issues, economics, technology, or culture, I’d love to build your first poll with you.

## LinkedIn post

Creators ask their audiences questions every day, but most social polls produce disposable numbers with almost no methodology or provenance.

Pollrr is our attempt to make audience polling both easy and defensible. Creators can publish and distribute polls free, respondents commit before seeing the live split, sources stay labeled, and the result carries a human sample size and verification record.

The AI boundary is deliberate: AI helps improve wording, identify themes, and communicate verified results. It never creates respondents or silently changes human totals.

We’re looking for an initial group of creators and independent publishers who want to turn audience questions into recurring, trustworthy content—not just another engagement widget.

## Editorial pitch

Subject: A human-first alternative to AI-generated public opinion

I’m building Pollrr, a free polling and audience-intelligence platform for creators and publishers. Its defining rule is that AI may help ask and explain, but only humans can vote. Respondents answer before seeing the result; recruitment sources and sample limitations remain visible; optional explanations reveal the reasoning behind each side; and signed aggregate records make published totals checkable without exposing respondent identities.

The timely story is not another poll generator. It is whether creators can become useful first-party audience researchers without reproducing the black-box weaknesses of social polls—or replacing people with synthetic respondents. I can offer a live test using a question chosen by your publication and show exactly what Pollrr does and does not claim.
