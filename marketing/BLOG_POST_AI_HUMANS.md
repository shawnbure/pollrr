# AI can explain a poll. It should not pretend to be a voter.

Artificial intelligence can draft questions, detect loaded language, cluster written explanations, and summarize a complicated result. Those are valuable capabilities. They do not make an AI model a respondent.

Pollrr uses a strict separation: human votes are measured records; AI output is an analytical layer.

That means an AI summary must state the verified human sample size. It must distinguish all respondents from the smaller group who supplied an optional explanation. It must show uncertainty and sampling limitations. It must not convert sentiment, generated personas, or model predictions into additional votes.

This distinction matters because synthetic data can look deceptively complete. A model can produce an answer for every demographic segment and every hypothetical question. Completeness is not measurement. If no person was recruited, shown the question, and recorded under a disclosed method, the output is an estimate—not a human response.

Pollrr AI is therefore built around four jobs:

1. Help creators ask a more neutral, comprehensible question.
2. Suggest useful follow-ups based on gaps in the existing human record.
3. Organize qualitative explanations without losing their denominator.
4. Turn verified aggregate results into clear, platform-ready communication.

If Pollrr later publishes modeled estimates, they will appear in a separate table with their model version, inputs, assumptions, and timestamp. Human totals will remain human totals.

The principle is simple: AI may help interpret public opinion. It may not impersonate the public.
